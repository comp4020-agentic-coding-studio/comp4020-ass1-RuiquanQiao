About the data

This page exists because the graph is only worth looking at if you can find out what is behind each line in it.

What counts as a relation

Two kinds, and only two, because these are the two that can be defined and sourced:

  • Academic supervision — Wikidata's P184 (doctoral advisor) and P185 (doctoral student).
  • Kinship — parent, child, spouse and sibling (P22, P25, P40, P26, P3373), kept only where both people are already in the graph.

Colleagues are deliberately absent. The first version of this project was going to include them. It doesn't, because "colleague" has no definition that survives contact with the data: everybody at the Cavendish was a colleague of everybody, no such line could carry a source, and a graph that looks well connected because of undefined edges is a made-up finding wearing a real number's clothes. A third kind of relation would need a definition and a source before it could be drawn.

How sure each line is

Wikidata claims vary in how well they are backed, and in the raw data a claim with three references looks exactly like a claim with none. That difference is printed on every relation rather than smoothed away:

  • n ref — Wikidata records at least one reference for it.
  • no reference — somebody entered the claim and nobody has cited anything for it. It is still shown, and still labelled.

What this graph does not know

  • Literature and peace are not here. The four prizes shown are physics, chemistry, medicine and economics.
  • Absence of a line is not absence of a relation. It means Wikidata has no claim for one. A laureate who lights up nothing may have stood alone, or may simply be poorly documented, and this page cannot tell you which.
  • Coverage is uneven by era and by place. Older European academic lineages are recorded in far more detail than recent ones or non-Western ones, so the shape of the tree is partly the shape of who has been written down.
  • Only ancestors were followed. Advisor chains were walked upward from each laureate. Students who never won anything are not here, so this is a picture of descent, not of influence.
  • One person appears twice. Wikidata holds two separate items for Henry De Wolf Smyth, and both turn up among Rutherford's students. They are not merged here. Deciding that two records are the same person is a claim, and making it on the strength of a similar name is how you eventually merge two different people who happen to share one.

This is not a replication

Richard Tol's The Nobel family (Scientometrics, 2024) reports that 696 of 727 laureates in these four categories belong to one academic family tree, with 668 descending from Emmanuel Stupanus, a Basel professor who argued against empirical evidence. That is a finding about Tol's dataset.

This page is built from a different source and reaches a different number of people. Every figure shown on the front page is counted from the snapshot this site ships with, at build time, so it cannot drift away from the file underneath it. The two claims are kept in separate sentences on purpose.

What this is

A student prototype for ANU COMP4020/8020, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Nobel Foundation. The data is a dated snapshot, not a live feed; nothing is fetched while the site is being built or while you are reading it.
